Wound Warning Signs

A healing wound should improve day by day (getting smaller, less painful, less fluid). If you notice any of the changes below, the wound may be getting worse or infected — it needs medical evaluation.

1. Changes in the wound itself

  • 🔴 The area of redness is spreading
  • 🔴 More swelling
  • 🔴 The area feels warm or hot
  • 🔴 More pain
  • 🟡 Yellow-green or pus-like drainage
  • 🟡 A clearly stronger bad smell
  • 🟠 The wound is getting bigger or deeper
  • ⚫ Wound edges turning black (may mean dead tissue)
  • 🔴 Red streaks spreading outward from the wound (may be a sign of lymphangitis)
  • 🟠 A wound that had healed splits open again

2. Whole-body symptoms (be even more alert)

  • 🌡️ Fever or chills
  • 😪 Feeling very tired or weak
  • 🧠 Confusion or a change in alertness
  • 🩸 Unusually high blood sugar (for people with diabetes)
  • 🍽️ A clear loss of appetite
  • ⚠️ Rapid worsening or feeling unwell all over

💡 Key reminder: Pain, worsening redness and swelling, pus, bad smell, and fever are all warning signs that need medical evaluation. Blackening wound edges may mean dead tissue. This site does not offer self-diagnosis of infection — this checklist is only meant to help you spot problems early.

If the wound is worsening quickly, you have whole-body symptoms,
or you simply aren't sure —
seek medical evaluation.
Not sure how serious it is? See When to Seek Care to decide between the ER, same-day care, or a clinic visit.
This content is general wound-care education. It cannot be used to judge the depth, infection, or treatment of an individual wound, and it cannot replace a doctor's examination.
