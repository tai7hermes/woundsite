When to Seek Care: Where Should You Go?

Three levels of urgency, from red to green: if you match a higher level, act on that level. When in doubt, treat it as the more urgent level.

🚨 Level 1: Emergency — call 911 or 119 / go to ER now

Action: Call 911 or 119, or go to the emergency room immediately
  • Bleeding that will not stop even with direct pressure
  • Blood spurting from the wound
  • A very deep or very large wound
  • A large object stuck in the wound (do not pull it out yourself)
  • A finger or limb that has lost feeling or cannot move
  • Severe burns or scalds
  • Confusion, trouble breathing, or signs of shock (pale face, cold sweat)
  • A wound spreading rapidly along with high fever or feeling very unwell

⚠️ Level 2: Same-day medical evaluation

Action: Same-day clinic or emergency evaluation
  • Human or animal bites
  • Puncture wounds or crush injuries
  • Wounds with dirt, saliva, or obvious contamination
  • Ongoing redness, swelling, warmth, and pain
  • Pus or a clear bad smell
  • Wound edges turning black
  • Any new wound on a diabetic foot
  • Any wound in a person with a weakened immune system
  • A wound that splits open or stitches that come out early
  • Not sure whether you need a tetanus shot

📅 Level 3: Book a clinic visit / follow-up

Action: Contact your usual care provider · keep your scheduled follow-up
  • The wound has not gotten smaller for a long time
  • Fluid keeps increasing
  • Dressings need changing more and more often
  • The wound keeps breaking open
  • The wound interferes with walking, sleep, or daily activities
  • You don't know what caused the wound
  • Your scheduled follow-up or stitch-removal date has arrived
