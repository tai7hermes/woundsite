About This Site

Methodology

  1. Content sources: only publicly available current guidelines from international wound-care societies (WHS, IWGDF, EPUAP/NPIAP/PPPIA, WOCN, EWMA, ISTAP, ISBI, SVS) and the primary literature they cite.
  2. Citation verification: every journal citation is individually checked against the official Crossref DOI registry and PubMed (DOI, authors, journal, volume/issue/year); institutional documents are verified for existence on official websites. Verification date: 2026-08.
  3. Update mechanism: each page shows its last-updated date; when a guideline is revised, pages are reviewed and revised one by one and recorded in the version history.
  4. Translation principle: Chinese summaries prioritize fidelity to the source; grading tables are summarized rearrangements of the original — for clinical use, please read the full original guidelines alongside.

Brand neutrality & conflict-of-interest statement

This site accepts no sponsorship from any dressing manufacturer. The "brand × category cross-reference" is for product identification only and does not constitute a ranking or purchasing recommendation. Society guidelines recommend dressing categories; adoption of a specific product should consider, in order: independent society guidelines → independent systematic reviews / comparative trials → local formulary and regulatory approval → the manufacturer's current IFU.

Disclaimer

The content of this site is an educational summary of clinical guidelines and cannot replace professional assessment and judgment for individual patients. Clinical decisions must integrate the patient's condition, local regulations, insurance coverage, and institutional policy. Wound Tracker App: transfer case photos with a USB drive

Before you start: USB drive — a USB-C drive plugs directly into the USB-C port at the bottom of an iPhone/Android phone; older USB-A drives need a USB-A to USB-C adapter. Recommended format: exFAT or FAT32 (readable on both Mac and Windows). Plug the drive into the phone's USB-C port, open the app and tap "Tracking" at the bottom to get ready to transfer case photos.

1. Upload (save case photos to the USB drive): ① open the app and tap "Tracking" ② tap the share icon (⬆️) on the right of the case to export ③ choose a format — encrypted package (.woundpkg) = photos + full records, requires a password (easy-to-remember digits), the recipient needs this app to import it; best for formal exchange and backup; plain viewing package (ZIP) = photos + PDF summary, NOT encrypted (requires ticking the unencrypted-risk option), viewable on any computer; for clinicians without the app ④ if you chose the encrypted package, the "Package password" page appears — enter and set a password (easy-to-remember digits are fine) ⑤ on the share sheet, tap "Share package", then "Save to Files" ⑥ when the USB drive's storage page appears, tap "Save" to upload the case photos ⑦ after the upload finishes, the "Case photos uploaded" page appears — tap "Confirm" ⑧ close the app before unplugging the drive.

2. Download (import case photos from the USB drive): ① plug the drive containing the encrypted package into the phone, open the app, tap "Tracking", then tap the download icon (⬇️) at the top right ② when the drive's storage page appears, select the .woundpkg package to import ③ when the "Package password" page appears, enter the password set at export and tap "OK" ④ the app verifies package integrity (8 checks); once passed it is imported as a NEW case — existing case photos are never overwritten ⑤ after the download finishes, the "Case photos downloaded" page appears — tap "Confirm" ⑥ the imported case then appears in the case list with all photos and records ⑦ close the app before unplugging the drive.

3. Notes: ① for formal exchange use the encrypted package (.woundpkg); the plain ZIP is NOT encrypted — anyone can view the photos if the drive is lost ② remember your password: a forgotten password means the encrypted package cannot be opened (no backdoor); easy-to-remember digits are fine ③ cases are named by nickname with no real names or identifiers; exported photos have phone info (EXIF) and GPS location automatically removed ④ re-importing the same encrypted package is detected to avoid duplicate cases ⑤ moving to a new phone: export an encrypted package to the drive on the old phone, then import it from the drive on the new phone — done.
